While Chanakya is traditionally remembered as a Vedic Brahmin and guru to Chandragupta Maurya, Jain traditions present a different perspective. They describe him not only as a Brahmin by birth but also as a devout, practicing Jain.The Jain connection to Chanakya (also known as Kautilya or Vishnugupta) is deeply rooted in historical texts:Jain Literature: The most prominent source for this is the Parishishta-Parvan (or Sthaviravali-Charita), an epic 12th-century CE text by the famous Jain scholar Acharya Hemachandra.Birth and Upbringing: According to these Jain texts, Chanakya was born in Chanaka village to parents Chanin and Chaneshvari, who were lay Jains (shravaka).Religious Devotion: The texts describe him as a learned practitioner of Jainism throughout his life. Jain monks are said to have prophesied his political rise even when he was a baby.Chandragupta's Faith: Furthermore, according to Jain traditions, his pupil Chandragupta Maurya eventually abdicated the throne, adopted Jain asceticism, and fasted to death (sallekhana) at Shravanabelagola in Karnataka under the guidance of Jain monks.For a deeper dive into the canonical texts and alternative historical accounts, you can explore the Wikipedia Chanakya Entry or read more about the Jain Traditions on Chanakya and Chandragupta.
